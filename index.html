The shoe decomposition
1,802 elite marathons · 2010–2024 · Jeremy Lee

How much of the post-2017 marathon speed-up is the shoe?

82seconds

95% CI 6–159 s · about 1.1% of a 2:05 marathon

Three separate methods estimate how much carbon-plated shoes took off median elite marathon time between 2010–2016 and 2018–2024. They land between 44 and 199 seconds, and pooled they come to about a minute and a half, about 1.1% of race time. The treadmill study behind the Vaporfly’s name measured a 4% cut in running energy cost, not in race time (Hoogkamer et al. 2018).

Updated 28 October 2026: wheelchair and handcycle results that the scraper had read as running times are now excluded. The pooled figure was 67 s before.

Three ways to hold the rest constant

The shoe arrived alongside better pacing, deeper East African fields and faster courses, so a plain before-and-after comparison credits the shoe with all of it. Each framework strips out a different part of that.

  1. Difference-in-differences. Road marathon improvement minus track 10,000 m improvement over the same window, rescaled to marathon time. Anything that lifted both events cancels.
  2. Within-athlete paired. The same runner’s career-mean time before and after 2017, which holds genetics and training history fixed. Not age-adjusted, because the race tables carry no ages.
  3. Cohort survival. The shift in the top-30 median between eras, with 55% attributed to shoes, plus a changepoint search on sub-2:10 and sub-2:25 counts.
